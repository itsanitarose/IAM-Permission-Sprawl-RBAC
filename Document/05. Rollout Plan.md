# 05. Rollout Plan

<br>

## Moving to RBAC

The roles are designed and the risks are identified. My next question was:

**How do I fix the access problems without stopping the Billing team from doing their jobs?**

I would roll out RBAC in three steps.

---

### Step 1. Secure Critical Access and Confirm Roles

**First priority: Fraser's access.**

Fraser has six billing and financial permissions he doesn't need for IT Support. I would work with IT to remove them urgently, while checking that essential technical support can continue.

For future troubleshooting, I would recommend **Just-in-Time (JIT) access**, using **Privileged Identity Management (PIM)** where supported.

| Stage | What Happens | Why It Matters |
|:------|:-------------|:---------------|
| **Request** | Fraser requests temporary elevated access for a specific support task. | No unnecessary permanent privileges. |
| **Approve** | An authorised person reviews and approves the request. | Independent oversight. |
| **Limit** | Access is restricted to approved technical permissions and a set period. | Least privilege. |
| **Record** | Administrative activity is logged. | Accountability and an audit trail. |
| **Expire** | Temporary privileges expire automatically. | Access does not remain active after the approved period. |

**Financial transactions remain outside his IT Support permissions.**

Before rolling out the new roles, I would also review them with the Billing, Finance, and IT managers to confirm what access employees need, discuss exceptions, and get their approval.

---

### Step 2. Test and Migrate

**Next priority: Make sure the new roles actually work.**

| Action | What I Would Do |
|:-------|:----------------|
| **Pilot** | Assign the new roles to a small group of users, temporarily keeping only the existing access needed for testing. |
| **Test** | Check that employees can create invoices, approve refunds, and complete the tasks required by their roles. |
| **Migrate** | Assign employees to their approved security groups and remove their old individual permissions. |
| **Check** | Look for any direct or inherited permissions that still give employees unnecessary access. |

I would complete the wider migration only after testing confirms that normal Billing work can continue.

---

### Step 3. Verify and Review

**Final priority: Make sure the changes stick.**

After migration, I would check:

- **Required access works:** Employees can still complete their normal tasks.
- **Unnecessary access is gone:** The permissions identified in my findings have been removed.

I would also recommend **access reviews every three months** to catch permission sprawl before it builds up again.

---



## My Recommendation

**Secure critical access first. Test the new roles before removing existing permissions. Verify the results and keep reviewing access.**

That is how I would introduce RBAC while reducing risk and keeping the Billing team working.
