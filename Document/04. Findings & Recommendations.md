# 04. Findings & Recommendations

<br>

## 1. Findings per Person

<br>

I compared each employee's current permissions with their job responsibilities to identify what access should be removed.

| Employee | Role               | Access to Remove                                               | Risk      |
| -------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Ana      | Billing Clerk      | ⚠️ Export all billing data                                     | Medium    |
| Ben      | Billing Clerk      | ⚠️ Approve refunds, Create new payee                           | High      |
| Chloe    | Billing Clerk      | ✓ None                                                         | No change |
| Dev      | Billing Supervisor | ⚠️ Create new payee, Approve payments, Export all billing data | High      |
| Erin     | Finance Manager    | ⚠️ Approve refunds                                             | Medium    |
| Fraser   | IT Support         | ⚠️ All seven billing and financial permissions                 | Critical  |

**Five of six accounts require access changes.** Chloe's permissions already match her job responsibilities.

*Risk ratings reflect my assessment of potential impact, not evidence of misuse.*

<br>


## 2. Most Serious Finding: Fraser

Fraser holds all seven permissions, combining **System Admin access with every financial permission** in the Billing application.

This stood out as the highest risk because someone with this level of access could potentially perform unauthorised financial actions while also controlling parts of the system used to manage or monitor them.


This combination creates three specific risks:

- **Insider fraud**

  Someone misusing Fraser's account could potentially create a fraudulent payee and approve payments without independent oversight, if the application doesn't enforce additional approval controls.

- **Concealing unauthorised activity**

  If System Admin access includes the ability to alter audit settings, logs or user permissions, the same person could potentially interfere with controls intended to detect misuse. The exact administrative capabilities still need verifying.

- **Account compromise**

  If Fraser's credentials were stolen through phishing, an attacker could inherit both his financial permissions and administrative access, increasing the potential damage from one compromised account.

<br>

**My recommendation:** Remove Fraser's six unnecessary billing and financial permissions and review the scope of his administrative access.

He should retain only the technical access needed to support the system.


<br>

## 3. My Assessment

I'd address Fraser's access first, followed by Ben and Dev because of their excessive financial permissions and potential SoD conflicts.

Ana and Erin also need corrections, while Chloe requires no changes.

**The priority is to address the highest risks without removing access employees genuinely need to do their jobs.**
