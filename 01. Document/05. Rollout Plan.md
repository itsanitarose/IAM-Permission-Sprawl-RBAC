# 05. Rollout Plan

## Moving to RBAC

With the new roles designed and the access risks identified, my next concern was **how to make these changes without disrupting the Billing team's work.**

I would approach the rollout in three steps.

### Step 1. Address Critical Access and Confirm Roles

**Fraser's access would be my first priority.** I would work with IT to urgently remove his six unnecessary billing and financial permissions, while checking that he can still provide essential technical support.

For tasks that require higher administrator privileges, I would recommend **Just in Time (JIT) access**, using Privileged Identity Management (PIM) where supported.

Fraser could request temporary administrator access for a specific support task. That request would need approval, the access would be limited to a set time, and his activity would be recorded. Once the approved time ends, the extra access would expire automatically.

**Temporary administrator access must not include permission to carry out financial transactions.**

I would also check the proposed RBAC roles with the Billing, Finance, and IT managers to confirm the permissions employees need and get their approval before the wider rollout.

### Step 2. Test and Migrate

I would test the new roles with a small group of users, temporarily keeping only the existing permissions needed to avoid disrupting their work.

I'd check whether employees can still create invoices, approve refunds, and complete the other tasks required by their roles.

Once testing confirms the new roles work correctly, I would assign employees to their security groups, remove the old individual permissions, and check for any remaining direct or inherited access.

### Step 3. Verify and Review

After migration, I would check each employee's actual permissions to confirm that unnecessary access has been removed and that the access they need still works.

I would also recommend reviewing permissions every three months to prevent permission sprawl from returning.

## My Recommendation

**Address critical access first, provide a safer way for IT Support to troubleshoot, and then move the wider team to RBAC without interrupting their work.**

*This is a proposed rollout for a fictional environment. No production changes were made.*
